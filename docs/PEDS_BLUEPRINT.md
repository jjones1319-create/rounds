# Pediatrics track: question blueprint (Blocks 4–8)

**Created:** 2026-10-16 · **Plan status:** approved by the owner 2026-10-16; pair 1 (B4-Q01, B4-Q02) pushed 2026-10-20 after passing the blind re-solve; pair 2 (B4-Q03, B4-Q04) pushed 2026-10-20 after passing the blind re-solve; pair 3 (B4-Q05, B4-Q06) written and fact-checked 2026-10-20 (the owner asked for four at once), waiting for the blind re-solve · **Rules:** `docs/board_question_generator_spec.md` (§2–§6) · **Loop:** `.claude/commands/peds-pair.md` writes two questions, `.claude/commands/peds-blind.md` re-solves them blind in a fresh conversation · **Packets:** `tools/board_review_packet.py`

A plan for 40 original pediatric board questions at USMLE Step 2 CK level and format, for patients from birth to 18 years, written as 20 pairs into five blocks of 8 (Blocks 4–8, `B4-Q01` to `B8-Q08`). Every ID is assigned here, before writing, and becomes permanent once pushed (saved progress on the phone is keyed by ID). Content is weighted by NBME's published Pediatrics Subject Examination content outline (systems, physician tasks, site of care) and checked against the Step 2 CK content outline; nothing comes from "high-yield" or recalled-exam lists. Every decision point is important (common, or dangerous and treatable), keyed to a firm recommendation ("should" or "should not"; strong or moderate; never a "may", a "consider" or a weak recommendation), and settled: the current US authorities agree on it. Each question also passes spec §2–§6: one defensible answer, checked numbers, five parallel options, every distractor a named error, full explanation, named and dated sources re-verified by web search at writing.

## 2. Calibration notes (official samples, read for structure, length and difficulty only)

Read: USMLE *Step 2 CK Sample Test Questions* (usmle.org PDF, 120 items, last modified August 2025; 26 items concern patients 18 or younger) and NBME *Pediatrics Sample Items* (nbme.org PDF, 19 items, last modified October 2025). Nothing from either was copied, paraphrased or adapted; the notes below are in our own words, and the scenario list exists so that no pediatric item in this plan uses one.

- **Vignette length.** Pediatric sample vignettes run about 60 to 250 words before the lead-in, with most between 75 and 190; the NBME shelf items are shorter on average (about 40 to 200, median near 90) than the Step 2 CK items (median near 130). Spec §4's 150–280 words sits at the long end; pediatric items should aim for 120–220 and never pad.
- **Opening.** Age with the unit the child's age calls for (days, weeks, months, or years), sex, who brings the child and where ("is brought to the office by his mother for a well-child examination", "is brought to the emergency department 30 minutes after…"), then the complaint with its timeline. Birth history, feeding, growth percentiles and immunization status appear only when they matter, in one clause each ("born at 40 weeks' gestation", "at the 60th percentile for height and 50th percentile for weight").
- **Vital signs** come in one sentence in the standard order (temperature in °C with °F, pulse, respirations, blood pressure, sometimes oxygen saturation), or as "Vital signs are within normal limits" when they do not matter.
- **Laboratory results** appear as a short vertical list or a two-column table with US conventional units; reference ranges are not printed except, rarely, in parentheses for an unusual test. Imaging and ECG findings are described in words, never shown. CSF and serum panels are given in full when the item turns on them.
- **Lead-ins** (verbatim, generic): "Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?" (the most common, 20 of 120 Step 2 CK items), "Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?" (14), "Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in diagnosis?" / "…in evaluation?", "Which of the following is the most appropriate initial pharmacotherapy?", "Which of the following is most likely to confirm the diagnosis?", "Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of these laboratory findings?", "Which of the following is the most likely cause of this patient's…?", "This newborn is at increased risk for developing which of the following…?", "Which of the following is the most appropriate initial response?" (communication). Two-step lead-ins exist ("In addition to…, which of the following…"), as spec §4 allows.
- **Options.** Five in most items (a few have four or six to eight; the NBME set includes one extended-matching set with ten lettered options). Options are short noun phrases of one to five words, parallel in form and alphabetical or logically ordered; drug options name the drug and route ("Intramuscular epinephrine"); test options name the test; management options are verbs plus object. Communication items use short quoted sentences.
- **Difficulty.** Most sample items turn on one or two findings (★★★ in spec §3 terms); the harder ones hide the decisive finding in the middle of the vignette or add a realistic red herring (a recent viral illness, a reassuring family history). None requires a calculation beyond a percentage or a simple ratio.
- **Mechanism items** are common in the pediatric samples (about one in five): they give the clinical picture and ask for the pathophysiology in a short phrase ("Infiltration of bone marrow by…", "Collagen abnormality"). That is why this plan includes six.

**Scenarios used by the official samples (do not use any of these as a vignette):** a toddler with repeated fractures after falls (abuse screening); an infant recovering after surgical repair of a ventricular septal defect; a school-age girl with long-standing asthma at a routine visit; an internationally adopted school-age boy with developmental and behavioral concerns; an adolescent boy with acute dyspnea and urticaria (anaphylaxis); a school-age boy with knee pain asking about therapy; a hospitalized school-age girl with a seizure and hyponatremia after trauma; an adopted toddler and advice about disclosing the adoption; an adolescent girl with postprandial epigastric pain (confirmatory test); an adolescent girl with lifelong short stature (next diagnostic step); a 5-day-old at the first visit after early discharge; a 1-week-old with an elevated TSH on newborn screening; an 18-year-old with migrating right lower quadrant pain; an adolescent boy with facial weakness, headache and CSF pleocytosis; a toddler with barky cough and stridor (croup, initial drug); an adolescent with cerebral palsy and situs inversus and a medical error; a 12-year-old girl at a well visit (screening study); a 12-month-old with fever and decreased appetite (next step); an adolescent skateboarder with a knee injury (activity and workup); a 14-year-old boy with behavior change and trespassing; a 6-year-old with acute flank pain and vomiting (stone); a toddler with cleft palate and heart defects (expected blood count finding); a 5-year-old with a linear itchy rash in summer (prevention); an 8-year-old with heat rash on vacation; a 1-week-old after myelomeningocele repair (associated finding). ## 3. Guidance that changed recently (each verified by web search on 2026-10-16; items key to the current version)

| Topic | Current version | What changed, and what it means for the items |
|---|---|---|
| Pediatric and neonatal resuscitation | AHA/AAP 2025 Guidelines for CPR and ECC (Circulation and Pediatrics, 22–23 October 2025); NRP 9th edition | Two-finger infant compressions eliminated (use two thumbs encircling the chest, or the heel of one hand if the chest cannot be encircled); infant choking: cycles of 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts, no abdominal thrusts; child choking: 5 back blows then 5 abdominal thrusts; newborn ventilation rate 30–60/min (was 40–60); deferred cord clamping for 60 s or more (was 30 s); a separate newborn "chain of care". B7-Q03 keys to the 2025 neonatal algorithm (compressions when the heart rate stays below 60/min after 30 s of effective ventilation; 3:1; epinephrine only after 60 s of compressions with effective ventilation), and the writer must take every number from Part 5 (doi 10.1542/peds.2025-074352), not from the 2020 text. |
| Hyperbilirubinemia in newborns ≥35 weeks | AAP clinical practice guideline (Kemper et al., Pediatrics, August 2022), replacing 2004 | Higher, gestational-age-specific phototherapy thresholds; neurotoxicity risk factors (isoimmune hemolysis, G6PD deficiency, sepsis or instability, albumin <3.0 g/dL) lower them; a new escalation-of-care threshold 2 mg/dL below the exchange threshold (NICU, intensive phototherapy, IV hydration, labs); urgent exchange transfusion for signs of intermediate or advanced acute bilirubin encephalopathy regardless of the level; DAT for infants of blood group O mothers who need phototherapy; follow-up timing set by the distance to the threshold. | Febrile infants 8–60 days | AAP clinical practice guideline (Pantell et al., Pediatrics, August 2021) | Three age strata with separate algorithms (8–21, 22–28, 29–60 days); inflammatory markers defined (temperature >38.5 °C, procalcitonin >0.5 ng/mL, ANC >4,000/µL, CRP >20 mg/L); the full evaluation with CSF and parenteral antimicrobials and admission for 8–21 days; CSF decisions in older strata depend on the markers; home management possible at 29–60 days with normal markers. ## 4. Excluded: decision points the current US authorities do not agree on, or that rest on weak recommendations

- The content of the childhood immunization schedule (routine versus shared decision-making), HPV dose count, COVID-19, RSV (nirsevimab) policy, influenza, rotavirus, hepatitis A, meningococcal, and the hepatitis B birth dose for HBsAg-negative mothers (CDC 2026 versus AAP 2026; litigation pending).
- Neonatal hypoglycemia thresholds and screening (AAP 2011 clinical report versus Pediatric Endocrine Society 2015).
- First-line treatment of newly diagnosed childhood ITP (ASH 2019: conditional, observation versus drugs).
- Transfusion strategy in acute chest syndrome (ASH 2020: conditional).
- Acute-phase aspirin dose in Kawasaki disease (AHA 2024; ACR 2021 allows either).
- Imaging after a first febrile UTI at 2–24 months (AAP 2011 guideline retired 2021; no successor).
- Bolus volumes and vasopressor choice in pediatric septic shock (Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2020: weak recommendations); use of adenosine before cardioversion in unstable SVT.
- Universal screening for iron deficiency at 12 months (AAP versus USPSTF), for scoliosis (USPSTF I), and universal lipid screening at 9–11 years (AAP versus USPSTF I).
- Bronchiolitis adjuncts (nebulized hypertonic saline, high-flow oxygen thresholds); croup dexamethasone dose (0.15 versus 0.6 mg/kg); asthma step-up details where NAEPP 2020 and GINA differ.
- Enuresis (no current US guideline), head lice products, specific stimulant choice in ADHD, concussion return-to-play timelines beyond "not the same day".
- Dosing specifics of vitamin D beyond 400 IU/day for infants; neonatal early-onset sepsis management beyond the shared categorical rule (reserve R8 only).
- Anything a sample item already uses (§2).

## 5. How the plan is used

- **Files.** Block files `questions/questions4.py` … `questions8.py` (lists `QUESTIONS4` … `QUESTIONS8`, created by the first pair of each block and appended by later pairs; same keys as `questions3.py` plus `status` and `reviewed_by`), imported and appended in `build.py`, each ID added to `SYS` with the label `Pediatrics` (one Systems chip and one Questions heading in the app). Per pair NN (01–20): `review/peds_pair_NN_sources.md` (verification log and design briefs), `review/peds_pair_NN_check.py` (every number re-derived), `review/peds_pair_NN.md` (key) and `review/peds_pair_NN_blind.md` (questions only), both from `python tools/board_review_packet.py questions/questionsN.py <id1>,<id2> <out> [--blind]`, and `review/peds_pair_NN_resolve.md` written by the blind re-solver (renamed `_resolve_1.md`, `_2.md` … if a round fails).
- **The loop.** One pair per conversation. `/peds-pair` (new conversation) takes the lowest-numbered pair whose rows are `planned`, verifies every decision by web search, writes both questions, runs the check script, the build and the three tests, writes the key and blind packets, and sets the rows to `checked`. `/peds-blind` (another new conversation that has not seen the pair) re-solves the blind packet, then compares with the key: agreement with no objection passes a question (`reviewed`); any disagreement fails it, the cause is fixed, the packets are regenerated and the pair is re-solved blind again. When both pass, the resolver updates spec §7, §9 and §11 and the board count in CLAUDE.md, shows the pair, and waits for the owner's OK before setting the rows to `pushed`, committing ("Add pediatric pair NN: <IDs>") and pushing.
- **Status.** Each row: `planned` → `written` → `checked` (fact-checked, packets written) → `reviewed` (passed the blind re-solve) → `pushed`. A question that cannot be made defensible is marked `rewrite` here with the reason, and `/peds-pair` writes it again from a reserve topic under the same ID (the ID never changes).
- **Reserves.** When a planned decision turns out to be outdated, contested or keyed to a weak recommendation, the writer takes a reserve topic that keeps the pair's system and age band different, uses the same ID, and logs the swap in the changelog.
- **Answer letters** are chosen at writing, not here (spec §5 balance per block).
- **Verification.** `/peds-pair` treats everything in this file as a lead: the guideline named in each row must be re-fetched at writing, its status confirmed (reaffirmed, revised or retired), newer versions searched for, and the other US bodies that speak to the decision checked (AAP, CDC/ACIP, AAFP, ACOG, AHA, IDSA/PIDS, NASPGHAN, ISPAD/ADA, ASH/NHLBI, AUA, ACR, ACEP).
