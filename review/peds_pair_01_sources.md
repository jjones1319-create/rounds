### Design brief

- **Decision tested.** A febrile (≥38.0 °C) well-appearing term infant aged 8–21 days gets the full evaluation (urinalysis by catheter, blood culture, lumbar puncture), parenteral antibiotics and admission, regardless of a normal urinalysis, normal inflammatory markers, or a viral exposure.
- **Detail that decides it.** Age 14 days (inside 8–21). The normal urinalysis, ANC 3,400/µL, CRP 0.6 mg/dL (6 mg/L) and the sibling's cold are the pull toward the 29–60-day pathway.
- **Most tempting wrong answer.** A, discharge home with reassessment in 24 hours: a good student knows the 2021 guideline lets a well-appearing infant with negative urinalysis and normal markers go home (KAS 20d) and may not remember that this applies only from 29 days (home management is also possible at 22–28 days under KAS 13a, but only after CSF is obtained and normal). Secondary pull: C (a dose of ceftriaxone and home, the old Boston/Philadelphia-style outpatient strategy) and E (get the procalcitonin before deciding).
- **One change that would make A right.** Make the infant 29 days old (or older): with a negative urinalysis and all markers normal, home observation without antibiotics and reassessment in 24 hours is a moderate recommendation (KAS 20d). So the item tests the age stratum, not recall.
- **Age band and system versus B4-Q02.** Neonate (14 days) / Multisystem versus infant (8 months) / GI: both differ.

### Design brief

- **Decision tested.** Suspected ileocolic intussusception is confirmed by abdominal ultrasonography before any reduction attempt; enema reduction follows confirmation in a stable child without peritonitis, free air or shock.
- **Detail that decides it.** The diagnosis is clinically likely but unconfirmed, and the child is stable with a soft, nontender-between-episodes abdomen: nothing forces either an immediate enema or surgery. The lethargy between episodes is the red herring (a pull toward lumbar puncture); it is dismissed by the afebrile state, flat fontanelle, arousability, and the abdominal mass with occult blood.
- **Most tempting wrong answer.** C, air-contrast enema: a good student knows the enema is "both diagnostic and therapeutic" and jumps to it. The ACR rates it *Usually Not Appropriate* as initial imaging; ultrasonography is *Usually Appropriate*, and the ACR–SPR parameter says the diagnosis is established by sonography before reduction.
- **One change that would make C right.** State that ultrasonography has already shown a concentric mass in the right upper quadrant (confirmed intussusception) in this stable child: then the air enema is the next step. A second change (rigid, distended abdomen or free air on a radiograph) would make surgery right instead; a third (fever, bulging fontanelle, lethargy without the abdominal findings) would make E right.
- **Age band and system versus B4-Q01.** Infant (8 months) / GI versus neonate / Multisystem: both differ.
