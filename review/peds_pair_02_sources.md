### Design brief

- **Decision tested.** When a convulsive seizure continues after two adequate benzodiazepine doses and the 20-minute mark, the next drug is a single full dose of a second-line agent (levetiracetam, fosphenytoin or valproate), not a third benzodiazepine, not phenobarbital while those are available, and not an anesthetic yet.
- **Detail that decides it.** Two full doses already given (IM midazolam 5 mg by the RAMPART rule for 13–40 kg at minute 10; IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg at minute 20) and 26 minutes elapsed; the interval change is that the seizure slowed after the first dose and resumed, and continued unchanged after the second.
- **Most tempting wrong answer.** C, a third benzodiazepine: students remember "lorazepam may be repeated" and may not count the prehospital midazolam. It would harm her: more than two doses triples the odds of respiratory depression (Chin 2008) and is associated with seizures lasting beyond an hour. Secondary pulls: D (phenobarbital, which many pediatric protocols still list) and A (intubation, because of the irregular breathing and the saturation of 94%).
- **One change that would make C right.** Give her only one adequate dose (e.g., no prehospital treatment, lorazepam in the ED at minute 10, still seizing at minute 15): a second full dose of IV lorazepam is then the AES step (may repeat once, level A).
- **Age band and system versus B4-Q04.** 1–5 years / Nervous system versus 12–18 years / Cardiovascular: both differ.
